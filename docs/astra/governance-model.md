# ASTRA Governance Model

## 3 Policies × 5 Governance Layers

ASTRA R&D uses three control policies across five governance layers. The model is a specification for experiments, not evidence of effectiveness by itself.

### Policies

#### P1 — Capability Policy

ASTRA may increase technical ability to reason, research, plan, use tools, adapt, and execute. Capability describes what the system can technically do.

#### P2 — Authority Policy

Increased capability or autonomy does not automatically increase authority. Every consequential action must be evaluated against explicit authorization.

#### P3 — Evidence Policy

Execution alone is not proof of successful control. Claims must follow a tested and evidenced observation.

`CLAIM → CONTROL → TEST → OBSERVE → AUDIT → EVIDENCE → RESULT CLAIM`

### Governance Layers

| Layer | Name | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Constitution | Fundamental objectives, prohibitions, constraints, authority boundaries, governance-change conditions |
| L2 | Authority | Converts principles into explicit delegated permissions: who may do what, under which conditions |
| L3 | Policy Engine | Deterministic evaluation of proposed action, context, risk, and authority: ALLOW / DENY / ASK / ESCALATE |
| L4 | Runtime Governance | Enforces the decision at execution: plan, check, execute, monitor, interrupt/stop |
| L5 | Evidence & Audit | Independently records and reconstructs objective, plan, decision, authority, capability, execution, observation, errors, approval, and outcome |

## Boundary

The agent/orchestrator may generate proposals. It does not self-authorize consequential actions and does not self-certify outcomes.

## Research Target

Test whether governance constraints remain invariant while capability and autonomy increase.
